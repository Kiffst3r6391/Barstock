## Quick start

1. **Check the pricing.** `pricing.md` currently quotes £1,000 purchase / £750 lease per year, both including stock setup. Change the figures there and in `one_pager.html` (licensing section) if they move.

2. **Print or attach the overview.** Either send `BarStock_Overview.pdf`, or open `one_pager.html` in Chrome or Safari and choose **File → Print → Save as PDF**, A4 portrait. The page is laid out to print to four sides: cover and problem, features and reports, what it looks like, then the case study, setup and pricing. If the figures change, regenerate the PDF rather than editing it.

3. **Send the email.** Use the short pitch in `emails.md`, attach the overview, and optionally the case study.

4. **Attach the feature sheet** when a buyer wants detail. It is Markdown; print it from any editor, or paste it into the body of a longer email.

## Notes on the look

- The overview uses Source Serif 4, Public Sans and IBM Plex Mono from Google Fonts. When printed or opened offline the fonts fall back to Georgia, the system sans and Menlo — close enough to the app's own fallbacks that the page still looks right.
- Colours are the app's tokens: paper `#F7F6F0`, ink `#1B221E`, bottle `#16443B`, copper `#8E5A2B`, rule `#D7D9CD`.
- Two screenshots carry the look, printed at A4 width. They use a separate demo database with invented figures and are labelled as illustrative — never the club's live sales or member names.

## Tips for selling to clubs

- **Lead with the problem, not the product.** Clubs care about wasted time, stock-outs and not knowing GP.
- **Offer a demo, not a brochure.** A 15-minute screen share is far more persuasive than a long email.
- **Use real numbers.** The external stocktake cost is the strongest one; the running surplus figure is the one committees ask about next.
- **Show the print.** Hand over the overview as paper if you meet in person; the printed style is part of the pitch.
- **Give a trial.** A 30-day trial with their own product list removes the risk.

## What to do next

After a few emails, note which parts clubs respond to — the stocktake saving, the GP visibility or the printed reports — and tighten the overview around that. Keep the overview to four sides; it is a taster, not a manual.
